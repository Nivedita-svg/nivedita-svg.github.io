Popularity isn’t always profit

Does the category that sells the most also make the most money? A category-level profitability analysis of the TheLook e-commerce dataset in Google BigQuery.

Context
Course group project
When
Fall 2026
Tools
SQL · BigQuery
Scope
26 categories

The question

Sales volume is the easiest number to watch, so it often stands in for “what’s working.” Our team tested that assumption: if you rank product categories by items sold, do you get the same list as when you rank them by profit and margin?

Key results

$121K
Profit from Outerwear & Coats in 2025, the highest of any category
55.6%
Outerwear & Coats margin, while it ranks 11th of 26 in items sold
#14
Profit rank of Intimates, the #1 category by items sold (46.8% margin)

Data

TheLook is a public e-commerce dataset in Google BigQuery for a fictitious clothing retailer. We worked at the category level: units sold, revenue, profit and margin for 26 product categories in 2025, plus profit growth from 2024 to 2025.

Approach

  • Wrote SQL queries to aggregate units, revenue, profit and margin by category.
  • Ranked categories by volume and by profit, and compared the two lists.
  • Followed up with profit growth from 2024 to 2025, to find categories growing more slowly than the company as a whole.

What we found

  • The volume leader isn’t the profit leader. Intimates sold the most items (2,240) at a 46.8% margin but ranked 14th in profit.
  • Outerwear & Coats led on profit with $121,201 at a 55.6% margin, despite ranking 11th in items sold.
  • Eleven categories grew profit more slowly than the company-wide rate from 2024 to 2025, from Plus (23.2%) up to Jumpsuits & Rompers (45.3%).

What this shows

Units sold alone doesn’t tell you where the business makes its money. Putting profit and margin next to volume gives a better basis for deciding which categories to prioritise, promote or review.
